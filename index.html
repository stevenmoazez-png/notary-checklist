Seller Signing Checklist

A field checklist for a residential seller closing — from package review to drop-off. Work it top to bottom; tap items as you go. Your progress is saved on this device.

The one rule everything else serves: you locate and identify, you never explain. Point at any line on any page. Say what a line means and you have crossed into practicing law.

Working a package you haven't seen? Photograph the pages and build a pre-signing brief first — phase 01 below.

Before you leave

At your desk — the signing is won or lost here

02

At the door

First five minutes — before a single page moves

Say this before you open the package

"I'm the notary — my job is to verify your identity, witness your signatures, and get this package back to escrow correctly. I'm not able to explain what any document means or give you advice; that's your escrow officer, and their number is right here. If anything comes up, we stop and call them. Take all the time you need."

This single paragraph prevents most of the trouble you'd otherwise face in the next hour.

03

The settlement statement — first

Money doc before anything else, so a dispute surfaces now and not at page 40

The sixty-second presentment

"This is your Estimated Seller's Statement from the title company. Sale price here. Your deductions run down this column — commissions, your loan payoff, excise tax, and your HOA charges including this line here. Bottom line, your estimated proceeds, right here. Note it says estimated. Take your time reading it, and anything you want explained, your escrow officer's number is at the top."

Then stop. Silence is the tool — it gives them room to react, and reacting is what you need them to do while there's still time to fix it.

06

Before you leave the table

Two minutes here saves a re-signing

Stop the signing

Any one of these. You do not need a second reason.

  • The signer is surprised by a number, or says some version of "nobody told me about that."
  • No acceptable ID, or a name mismatch you cannot resolve.
  • The signer can't read the documents themselves — you are not permitted to interpret or translate.
  • The signer seems confused, impaired, or medicated, or can't tell you in their own words what they're doing.
  • Someone else is answering for them, pressuring them, or won't leave the room when asked.
  • Pages are missing, or the notarial certificate doesn't match the act required.
  • You're asked to leave a blank, backdate, or notarize for someone who isn't present.
  • The signer insists you explain something and won't accept the escrow referral.

What stopping looks like: stay calm, stay in the chair. "I want to make sure this is right before you sign it — let me get your escrow officer on the phone." You are never the person who decides whether the closing proceeds. You're the person who makes sure nobody signs something blind.

Say this, not that

Memorize four of these and you will never be the notary who got a complaint filed

"That's a question for your escrow officer — here's the number. I'm happy to wait while you call."

Your default answer to anything substantive

"I can show you where that is on the page, but I'm not allowed to tell you what it means."

When they point at a line and ask what it is

"I need you to sign exactly as your name is printed here."

Every signature line, every time

"I'd rather stop and get this right than have you sign something you're unsure about."

When they hesitate — gives them permission to pause

"I'm not able to advise you on that, and I'd be doing you a disservice if I guessed."
